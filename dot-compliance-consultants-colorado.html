Do you need a compliance consultant in Colorado?

Most small carriers manage compliance themselves until something makes it harder to keep up: a new entrant safety audit, a CSA warning letter, driver files that have fallen behind, or simply not enough hours in the day. An independent DOT compliance consultant can review your records, help you prepare for an audit, or run your drug and alcohol program for you. It's optional, and plenty of carriers in Colorado never need one.

Interstate vs. intrastate authority in Colorado

If you operate across state lines, you're regulated primarily by FMCSA under your USDOT number: new entrant safety audits, hours of service, drug and alcohol testing and the rest of 49 CFR apply the same way in every state. If you operate only within Colorado, you may also need separate state operating authority or permits on top of that. Exactly what's required, and how it's changed recently, is Colorado's call to make — not ours.

Colorado's own source

Colorado Department of Revenue, DMV – International Registration Plan is the official state agency for motor carrier regulation in Colorado. Confirm any state-specific registration, permit or intrastate authority requirement directly with them before you rely on it. Port of Entry weigh stations and oversize/overweight permits are run by the Colorado State Patrol; household goods/passenger authority is regulated by the Colorado PUC.
