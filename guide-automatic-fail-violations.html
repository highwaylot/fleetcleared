The 16 violations that fail your safety audit automatically

Short answer

FMCSA lists 16 regulations that fail a new entrant safety audit on their own. Fourteen fail you on a single instance; missing yearly truck inspections and missing hours-of-service logs fail you when they show up in half or more of the records checked.

All 16, most common first

FMCSA doesn't publish counts for each rule. The order below comes from consultant and industry reports, which consistently put drug and alcohol testing at the top.

#What fails youWhat it meansHow to avoid itHow common
1No random drug and alcohol testing program
49 CFR 382.305 · One instance		Your CDL drivers, including you if you drive, aren't in a random testing pool.Join a consortium or set up your own random pool before your first CDL driver works.Most common
2No drug and alcohol testing program at all
49 CFR 382.115 · One instance		No testing program was in place when a CDL driver started working.Have a written policy and a negative pre-employment drug test before anyone drives.Most common
3Trucks without a yearly inspection
49 CFR 396.17(a) · 51% or more of records checked		No annual inspection record for the vehicles checked.Inspect every truck and trailer at least once every 12 months and keep the report.Common
4Drivers not keeping hours-of-service logs
49 CFR 395.8(a) · 51% or more of records checked		Drivers aren't required to record their hours.Use an ELD or paper logs unless a written exemption applies, and keep the records.Common
5Driver without a valid medical card
49 CFR 391.11(b)(4) · One instance		A driver isn't medically qualified.Keep a current DOT medical certificate for every driver and track expiry dates.Common
6Operating without the minimum insurance
49 CFR 387.7(a) · One instance		Liability coverage is below the federal minimum for what you haul.Confirm your limits with your agent. General freight is usually $750,000.Occasional
7Driver-reported defect not fixed
49 CFR 396.11(c) · One instance		A driver wrote up an out-of-service defect and the truck ran again before repair.Repair it, sign the report, and keep it before the truck moves.Occasional
8Driving a truck put out of service
49 CFR 396.9(c)(2) · One instance		A truck ordered out of service at an inspection moved before it was repaired.Repair first, then return the signed inspection report as instructed.Occasional
9Driver without a valid CDL
49 CFR 383.3(a) / 383.23(a) · One instance		A driver needs a CDL for the vehicle and doesn't have one.Check the license class and endorsements against every vehicle they drive.Occasional
10Using a disqualified driver
49 CFR 391.15(a) · One instance		A driver is disqualified under driver-qualification rules.Pull a driving record at hire and every year, and act on what it shows.Rare
11Using a driver disqualified from holding a CDL
49 CFR 383.51(a) · One instance		A driver lost CDL privileges, for example after a DUI.Same as above: driving records at hire and yearly.Rare
12Using a driver whose license is suspended or revoked
49 CFR 383.37(b) · One instance		You knew, or should have known, the license wasn't valid.Same as above, plus a written rule that drivers report suspensions.Rare
13Using a driver who tested positive for drugs
49 CFR 382.215 · One instance		A driver kept working after a positive test.Remove them from driving until they finish the return-to-duty process.Rare
14Using a driver who refused a test
49 CFR 382.211 · One instance		A refusal counts like a positive, and the driver kept working.Treat a refusal exactly like a positive result.Rare
15Using a driver with alcohol at 0.04 or higher
49 CFR 382.201 · One instance		A driver worked at or above 0.04 alcohol concentration.Remove them from driving and follow the return-to-duty process.Rare
16Passenger vehicle without the minimum insurance
49 CFR 387.31(a) · One instance		A bus or passenger van ran below the required coverage.Passenger carriers only: confirm limits for your seating capacity.Rare (passenger only)

If your drivers don't need a CDL

Seven of the 16 only apply to carriers with CDL drivers: the five drug and alcohol rules and the CDL rules. If you run box trucks under 26,001 pounds without placarded hazmat and without CDL drivers, the drug and alcohol testing rules in Part 382 don't cover you. Driver files, medical cards, logs, inspections and insurance still do.
